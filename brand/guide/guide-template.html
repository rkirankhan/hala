01 · The brand

An AI Employee, not a chatbot.

Hala answers every call, message and enquiry on the first ring, books customers in, follows up, and hands over to your team when someone needs a person. That sentence is the brand — every piece of marketing is some part of it made concrete.

The name

هالة means halo. The name also lands as a greeting — "Hala!" is how you welcome someone in much of the Arabic-speaking world, and it sounds close to "Hello". A name about answering warmly, worn by a product that answers. In every language, the brand is written Hala in Latin script — including inside German and Arabic sentences.

Who it speaks to

Owners of busy local businesses — restaurants, takeaways, salons, clinics, trades — who lose customers every day to an unanswered phone. They are practical, time-poor and allergic to tech hype. The brand speaks to them in their working day: Friday 19:42, mid-service, plating desserts. Never in AI-industry language.

Positioning spine

The core promise is industry-neutral — customers, enquiries, appointments. Sector-specific words (covers, allergens, stylists, patch tests) belong only in industry-scene content, one sector at a time. This is how the site is written, and marketing follows the same rule: adding an industry never changes the core message.

Product and parent

Hala is a Khaas Hub product. The lockup is "Hala by Khaas Hub" — Hala leads, the byline stays quiet and never grows to equal weight. On social profiles, Hala gets its own accounts; Khaas Hub appears in the byline, the about text and nowhere else.

The one-sentence test. If a post can't be traced back to "answers on the first ring, books, follows up, hands over", it's off-brand — however clever it is.
02 · Logo

An open ring, and a reply.

The mark is an open ring — هالة means halo — with a dot resting where the ring opens. The gap reads as a speech tail, the dot as the reply. The dot is the same green the product uses for live status: by the time someone has seen the brand twice, they know what that green means.

The 90° gap is centred at the bottom so both ends land at the 4 and 8 o'clock positions — where a headset's earcups sit. The double reading is deliberate: a halo to anyone who knows the name's meaning, a headset to everyone else. One end carries the dot; the other is left plain. Two dots would make it just a headset; none would make it just a ring.

Clear space & minimum size

Keep clear space around the mark equal to half the ring's diameter on every side. Minimum sizes: 20px for the small cut, 24px for the standard cut, 32px for the lockup. As an avatar, the mark sits alone at roughly 60% of the tile on the Night background — never the lockup, which dies in a small circle.

Don't

  • Don't "correct" the gap into a tidy speech-bubble opening or close it into a full circle — the 4-and-8-o'clock opening is the whole idea.
  • Don't recolour the dot. It is Live Green because green means "live" everywhere in the product. An indigo dot is a different brand.
  • Don't add a second dot, rotate the mark, outline it, add gradients or drop shadows, or set it in a container shape.
  • Don't put the colour mark on busy photography or mid-tone backgrounds — switch to mono white/black instead.
  • Don't redraw the wordmark. It is always Plus Jakarta Sans 700 with -0.01em tracking, white or near-black.
03 · Colour

Dark-first, one accent, and a green that means something.

Rules

  • Roughly 90% of any canvas is Night, Panel and type. Indigo is an accent, not a fill — if a design feels "very purple", it's wrong.
  • One indigo word per headline. The site emphasises exactly one word ("lead", "first ring") — never whole sentences.
  • Green only ever means live / confirmed / booked. Don't decorate with it; it's the payoff colour that shows the job got done.
  • Light backgrounds are the exception (print, co-branding). On white: Night for type, Deep Indigo as accent, and the same green rule.
  • Contrast: body text on dark is Muted or lighter; small indigo text uses #8E9BFF. Never Faint below 12px.
04 · Typography

Jakarta speaks. Inter explains.

Two families, one job each. Plus Jakarta Sans carries every headline, price and UI label — it holds its weight from hero size down to a card title without turning fragile or fashion-editorial. Inter carries body copy. A system monospace carries eyebrows and metadata.

Rules

  • Tracking follows optical size: −0.025em at 32px+, −0.02em at 22–31px, −0.01em below 22px. One negative value everywhere makes small text cramped.
  • Weights: 700 for display, 600 for headings and UI, 400–500 for body. Nothing lighter than 400 on dark.
  • Sentence case everywhere except mono eyebrows, which are uppercase with +0.13em letter-spacing.
  • Headlines end with a full stop when they're statements — it's part of the plain-spoken register.
  • Arabic uses IBM Plex Sans Arabic inside the same stacks — Latin words, including "Hala", keep the Latin face inside an Arabic sentence.
  • Fallbacks when the brand fonts are unavailable (e.g. plain-text platforms): system sans. Never substitute a serif or a rounded display face.
05 · Voice & tone

Plain-spoken, concrete, and on the owner's side.

Hala sounds like a sharp, calm colleague explaining something over the counter — not a SaaS landing page. The register is British, direct and specific. If a sentence could appear in any AI company's deck, it doesn't belong here.

Principles

  • Scenes, not claims. Start where the reader is standing: "Friday, 19:42. Everyone's mid-service." A time and a situation beat any adjective.
  • Numbers do the talking. First ring. 60 seconds. 24 hours. Live in 48 hours. Never "revolutionary", "seamless", "game-changing", "supercharge".
  • Second person, their world. "Your hours, your services, your prices, your policies." The product is described by what it takes off the owner's plate.
  • An employee, not a bot — but never a secret. Hala is written about like a capable colleague ("it answers", "it books", "hands over"). At the same time, customers must always be told they're talking to an AI. Warm, never deceptive.
  • Short sentences with rhythm. "1 employee. 6 channels. Every hour of the day." Fragments are fine. Exclamation marks are not — the brand almost never uses them.
  • Honest to a fault. No invented stats, no fake testimonials, no integration we don't have. The site holds its proof section empty until a real customer will be named; marketing does the same.

Hype adjectives, industry jargon, exclamation marks, and nothing the reader can picture.

German

German copy is a rewrite, not a translation — idiomatic first, faithful second. Register is Sie throughout, without exception; the buyer is a business owner and du reads as presumptuous. Where English uses "it", German names Hala as the subject ("Hala antwortet…") to sidestep the er/sie pronoun choice. Times are 24-hour (14:15), thousands use a dot (1.200), prices are in euros. Everything German should get a native speaker's pass before publishing — especially headlines, where English rhythm rarely survives word-for-word.

Claims discipline. Three claims are off-limits until the facts change: an agent that switches language per caller (each deployment is one language), integrations not yet confirmed, and any customer numbers or testimonials. When in doubt, describe behaviour, not results.
06 · Social media

Show the phone being answered.

The strongest thing Hala can post is the product doing its job: a conversation, a booked outcome, a green light. Social content recreates the site's rhythm — dark canvas, one idea per card, one accent word, the URL in the footer.

Template rules

  • Every card carries the mark + wordmark top-left and hala.khaashub.com in the footer, separated by a hairline.
  • One indigo word per headline, chosen for meaning ("lead", "first ring") — the accent moves to wherever the emphasis falls in German.
  • Chat cards show real product behaviour only — exchanges the agent genuinely handles, guest in Inset grey on the left, Hala in indigo wash on the right, outcome card with the green check underneath.
  • The "Answering now" live badge (green dot + mono caps) is reserved for content showing the product live — don't use it decoratively.
  • The oversized ghost ring sits at ~5% white opacity, cropped by a corner — never fully visible, never in colour.
  • Photography, when it arrives, is real businesses mid-service in low warm light — never stock handshakes, headsets or robots. No robot imagery, ever.
